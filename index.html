Climate Change Extends Fire Season

Climate change is fundamentally altering fire seasons in this region. Temperatures are rising. Rainfall is becoming less predictable. Droughts last longer. Vegetation stays drier for more months of the year. This means fire season is getting longer and more intense. What used to be June through October is now May through November or even December. Some years skip normal patterns entirely. A warm, dry winter might bring fires before spring. A cool, wet spring might create a false sense of safety, then a hot June brings massive fires. As a homeowner, assume fire risk year-round. Do not let any month lower your guard. Maintain your defensible space and emergency preparedness every single month. Do not get complacent in December or January. If your area experiences a warm spell, fire risk returns immediately. Your attention to fire safety must be constant.
